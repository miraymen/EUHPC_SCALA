Scaling Laws and a Foundation Model for Human Motion

What if motion data came from a camera that takes text prompts?

Abstract

Human motion synthesis generates realistic human movement in 3D environments for animation and games, and robotics now depends on it. Synthesized motion can be retargeted onto humanoid robots, which then perform tasks in factories, warehouses and homes without manual programming of each behaviour. Current methods train on only 40 hours of motion capture (AMASS), which limits them to simple walking and gesturing. Motion synthesis today is where language modelling was before large-scale data: the models exist, but the data does not. ScaLa tests whether a text-prompted video generator, followed by pose estimation and physics stabilization, can replace motion capture as a source of 3D human motion. The project answers four measurable questions: (1) whether the recovered motion matches capture-grade quality; (2) whether motion models obey scaling laws, and how many synthetic hours replace one real hour; (3) which properties of the video generator transfer to the motion model; and (4) whether the motion knowledge transfers to humanoid control. Answering them requires 35,000 hours of generated motion (875 times AMASS) and a model family from 100M to 16B parameters. The project requests 90,000 node-hours (360,000 GPU-hours) on the JUPITER Booster. Data generation uses 25% of the allocation, and training and fine-tuning use 75%. The dataset, the scaling laws and the model family will be released openly.

Motivation

Motion data is the bottleneck

Motion synthesis today is where language modelling was before large-scale data: the models exist, but the data does not.

Humanoid robots now learn behaviours from human motion: recent work retargets captured or synthesized motion onto robot skeletons and executes it through sim-to-real transfer. The motion models that generate this motion therefore limit what robots can do. Current models cannot reproduce the full range of human motion that real deployment needs, such as walking sideways through narrow gaps, manipulating objects at different heights and crossing cluttered factory floors.

The main obstacle is data scarcity. Motion synthesis depends on a single dataset, AMASS: about 40 hours of optical motion capture with roughly 300 action types, recorded in laboratories. With our tokenizer (30 frames per second, 4 tokens per frame), AMASS holds 17 million tokens. GPT-3 uses about 300 billion training tokens. Capture needs studios, suits, calibrated cameras and actors, and its cost grows with every recorded hour. ScaLa turns data collection into computation, the problem that an AI Factory solves.

Plan

Four questions, four work packages

One hypothesis: generative video models can replace motion capture as the measurement instrument of human motion research.

RQ1 · O1 · WP1

Can generative video models replace motion capture as a source of 3D human motion data?

Build and validate the instrument: video generation, pose estimation and physics stabilization. Release 35,000 hours of motion.

RQ2 · O2 · WP2

Do motion models obey scaling laws, and does synthetic data bend them?

Measure the first scaling laws for motion synthesis over 100M to 16B parameters and 150 to 35,000 hours.

RQ3 · O3 · WP3

Which properties of the video generator transfer to the learned motion model?

Compare LTX-2.3 and Wan2.2 on a fixed prompt subset with identical motion models.

RQ4 · O4 · WP4

Does motion knowledge distilled from video transfer to embodied control?

Train and release the model family, fine-tune it with reinforcement learning and retarget it onto humanoid robots.

WP1 · RQ1

The measurement instrument

A generator renders a clean view of a person who performs a prompted action. Pose estimation and physics stabilization turn that view into 3D motion.

Two observations make it possible to generate motion data at large scale without motion capture hardware. First, video generative models learn motion from messy web video and render it in clean, controlled settings: static camera, no occlusion, fully visible body. Monocular 3D pose estimators are most accurate in exactly this setting. Second, pose estimation and physics stabilization extract stable and diverse 3D motion from these videos, and this motion trains a foundation model for human motion.

Every stage measures or enforces the quality of its output. A threshold on pose-estimation confidence rejects clips with generation artefacts. The physics tracker follows each sequence in simulation, and the pipeline discards every sequence that it cannot track, so every retained motion is physically executable. On about 4,000 internet videos, a physics controller can track 82.5% of the motion from FlowHMR, against 62.8% for GVHMR. Held-out motion capture from AMASS validates the data through distributional metrics and downstream task performance.

WP2

Motion as tokens

A VQ-VAE tokenizer turns each frame into 4 tokens, so the full dataset holds 15.1 billion tokens.

WP2 · RQ2

Scaling laws for motion

Eight model sizes, six dataset scales and three seeds: the first scaling laws for motion synthesis.

Autoregressive transformers of eight sizes (100M to 16B parameters) train on six dataset scales (150 to 35,000 hours) with three seeds each, using PyTorch FSDP. Each run trains on 20 tokens per parameter, the compute-optimal ratio, and stops at 32 passes over its data. The 16B run on the full dataset belongs to WP4a, which leaves 141 grid runs.

The fits give three results. The scaling laws let a group read the expected quality for its data and compute budget off a curve, as language modelling does today. The synthetic-to-real exchange rate says how many synthetic hours one real hour is worth. Because the pipeline can generate new data at any time, the repeated passes also measure when new data beats repetition. Data arrives in batches, and the project refits the law after each batch: if the law saturates before 35,000 hours, generation stops early and the unused hours go to training.

WP3 · RQ3

Which generator properties transfer?

The same pipeline on two video generators of different fidelity isolates how much motion quality depends on the generator.

WP4 · RQ4

Foundation model and embodied transfer

A robot learns by imitation only the behaviours in its motion prior. ScaLa widens that prior.

The 16B model trains on the full 35,000-hour dataset with the configuration that the WP2 grid selects. Three seeds of 320 billion tokens each (21 passes) complete the top corner of the grid and give the released model. PPO with physics rollouts in ProtoMotions then fine-tunes the 2B, 8B and 16B models to follow scene constraints (paths, contacts, obstacles). Three tasks, three seeds and three model sizes give 27 runs, which measure whether embodied transfer improves with model size. Retargeting experiments map generated motion onto humanoid robot skeletons.

The programme needs 360,000 GPU-hours within six months, which equals about 82 GPUs in continuous use. A typical 32-GPU institutional cluster would need about 15 months at full use. The 16B runs also need 64 nodes (256 GPUs) on one fast interconnect for days. Average use is about 21 nodes, roughly 0.3% of the 6,000-node JUPITER Booster, and the peak is 128 nodes.

Q1 (months 1 to 3). Deployment check, final action taxonomy and prompt generator. Video generation runs on about 12 nodes, and pose estimation and physics stabilization process the completed batches. The full 35,000 hours are ready in month 3. The scaling-law grid runs on the dataset scales up to 15,000 hours as the data arrives, and the generator ablation is completed.

Q2 (months 4 to 6). The grid is completed at 35,000 hours. The 16B foundation model trains on the full dataset. RL fine-tuning, evaluation and robot retargeting follow. The dataset, model weights and code are released.

Outcomes

What the allocation delivers

RQ1 and RQ2 are measurements, so the project produces a decision-grade answer even if the hypothesis fails.

O1

Instrument and dataset

The validated generation pipeline, and 35,000 hours of physically stabilized human motion in SMPL format with hands and text annotations, released on Hugging Face with full generation provenance.

O2

Scaling laws

The first scaling laws for motion synthesis, the synthetic-to-real exchange rate and the value of repeated data.

O3

Generator transfer

A measured answer to which properties of a video generator transfer to the learned motion model.

O4

Foundation model and embodied transfer

Released model weights (100M, 300M, 0.5B, 1B, 2B, 4B, 8B and 16B parameters), RL-fine-tuned scene-conditioned synthesis in 3D environments, and retargeting onto at least one humanoid robot platform.

The pipeline follows the data-governance and privacy principles of the EU AI Act by construction. It uses no real human footage and processes no personal data. The prompt taxonomy controls the composition of the dataset, so demographic attributes, action categories and environments are balanced and auditable. The dataset records the full generation provenance of every sequence: prompt, generator and filters applied.

Notes

About the animations

  • The animations explain the plan; they are not results. Every number in them is quoted from the proposal, and rates and scaling efficiencies are measured on JUPITER under Benchmark Access. The rest is illustration: the losses, exponents, exchange rate and generator balance that ScaLa will measure, the gate rejects and raw estimates, the decoded motion, the reinforcement-learning rollouts and the provenance record.
  • Clips shown large are real web videos that stand in for generated clips, so that the motion on screen is physically correct; their sources are listed below. Small background panes show clips from open video generators (LTX-2, Wan2.2 and Wan2.1 VACE-14B).
  • 3D bodies: the people who sit down and the body that covers the person in a clip are lifted from the clips with NLF (Neural Localizer Fields), aligned to the floor, smoothed and rendered with the SMPL body model; seats are built at the height measured from the lifted hips. The proposal uses FlowHMR and PHC for these stages. All other bodies (the behaviour ring, the motion tiles and fields, the tokenizer, the robot dioramas) are captured motions from AMASS and HPS, and the clips that fly out of the JUPITER nodes are generated clips.
  • Robot footage: SONIC, NVIDIA (Science Robotics 2026), from its project page, credited wherever it appears.
